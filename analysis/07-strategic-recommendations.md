# Phase 7 — Strategic Recommendations + Advanced Extensions

> *Specific plays for authors, publishers, universities, governments, enterprises — plus the cross-cutting structural forces (ISBN, Amazon, GitHub, certification, AI-generation risk, open access).*

The strategic frame across all five audiences: **the next five years of technical publishing is won by entities that pick a defensible position on the trust × velocity × language matrix and double down — not by entities that try to hold every position.**

## 1. Recommendations for Authors

### 1.1 Where to write
- **Tier-1 priority** (high return, durable, BCQ-anchored):
  - The Post-Quantum Migration Playbook ([`05` #38](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) — the single highest-COS slot in the entire field.
  - Cryptographic Agility ([`05` #39](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) — the structural twin of the migration playbook.
  - Tokenizing Real-World Assets ([`05` #73](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) — the largest unfilled slot in finance-tech publishing.
  - The Quantum-Ready Enterprise ([`05` #32](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) — the *commercial* quantum book the market wants.
  - Smart Contract Security by Design / Audit Engineering Handbook ([`05` #64–#65](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)).
  - AI-only Tier-1 candidates (not numbered in BCQ-focused [`05`](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md) but discussed in [`04`](./04-market-gaps.md)): senior agentic-systems engineering, AI assurance, inference economics, AI-native software engineering.
- **Tier-2 priority** (strong, audience-specific, BCQ-anchored):
  - Engineering a Quantum Compiler ([`05` #1](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) — generational textbook slot.
  - Building L2 Rollups ([`05` #68](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) and zkVM Engineering ([`05` #56](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)).
  - PQC for Embedded and IoT ([`05` #42](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) — defensible, regulator-aligned niche.
  - EUDI Wallet & Verifiable Credentials Engineering ([`05` #91](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) — eIDAS 2.0 mandate.
  - AI for Quantum Control ([`05` #94](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) — the *real* AI×Quantum intersection.
- **Avoid** (saturated, low return):
  - Generic *Build with X chatbot* books.
  - Beginner Python + ML.
  - Beginner Solidity / smart contracts.
  - Beginner Qiskit / quantum tutorials.
  - Generic prompt engineering.
  - Generic blockchain / cryptocurrency primers.

### 1.2 Format choice (match book to topic maturity)
- **Emergent topic + thinning research front:** publish a *survey monograph* (Now Publishers F&T format) rather than a full book. Smaller commitment, citable.
- **Growth topic + framework churn:** publish a *senior practitioner playbook* that explicitly separates principles from API specifics. Survives multiple framework generations.
- **Mature topic + curriculum demand:** publish an *opinionated textbook* with classroom support. 5-10 year shelf life.
- **Regulation-driven topic:** publish a *practitioner handbook + cert-prep* combo. Buyer cohort is mandated; revenue is durable.

### 1.3 Authorship economics
- Trade-publisher advance + royalty math beats self-publishing only if the topic has *strong audio/video/course extensions* the publisher will activate. Otherwise, modern self-publishing economics (Leanpub, Stripe Press model, Substack-to-book) often dominate.
- **The Substack-to-book pipeline is the single most important authorship asset of 2026-2030.** Building 5-25k engaged readers on a focused technical Substack creates leverage over publishers, agents, and one's own pricing. Publishers actively bid for proven Substack authors.
- **AI co-authoring should be disclosed and quantified.** "Drafted with AI assistance, edited and fact-checked by author" is now a standard disclosure expectation. Going forward, *non-disclosure* is a brand-risk event.

### 1.4 Author positioning (the "voice" question)
- *Opinionated > balanced.* The market is saturated with surveyish books. Books that take a clear position on disputed engineering questions outperform.
- *Senior practitioner narrators* (10+ years of relevant experience, demonstrated production-system ownership) are the highest-trust voice in the agentic-AI / AI-assurance / inference-engineering categories.
- *Author bundles* — two senior practitioners co-authoring — outperform solo books in the senior-engineering tier.

### 1.5 Career arc plays
- For an early-career author, a focused 6-9 month book on a *Tier-2* topic with a clear publisher lane is a better career investment than a sprawling Tier-1 book that may not ship.
- For a mid-career engineer, the *senior practitioner playbook* in their specialty (Tier-1) is a generational career asset — equivalent to a successful conference talk circuit.
- For an academic, the path is now: paper → workshop volume → F&T survey → graduate textbook. Skipping the survey-monograph step is a common error.

## 2. Recommendations for Publishers

### 2.1 By publisher archetype

**Front-list velocity publishers (O'Reilly, Manning, Packt):**
- Cede the beginner shelf to self-pub and AI.
- Concentrate on senior-practitioner playbooks (O'Reilly), in-action engineering (Manning), and vendor-anchored cookbooks (Packt).
- Multi-language commissioning is the single highest-ROI growth lever — Packt is best positioned today, Manning is in second place.

**Canonical / academic publishers (MIT, CUP, OUP, Springer, Pearson-AW):**
- Commission *generational* textbooks now, with 2027-2029 publication targets. The next *Goodfellow*-class anchor textbook in deep learning, the next *Russ-Tedrake*-class anchor textbook in robotics-AI, the next *Hennessy-Patterson*-class anchor textbook in AI systems — these *will* exist. The question is which press anchors each.
- Use proceedings + workshop volumes as monograph funnels. Springer is structurally best at this; ACM Books is closing the gap.

**Hybrid publishers (Wiley, Apress, CRC):**
- Specialize per topic. Wiley owns regulated-industry AI (finance, healthcare, insurance, pharma). CRC owns industrial / engineering / signal AI. Apress is the natural home for *practitioner-meets-research* hybrids and is uniquely positioned to anchor the AI-assurance series.
- *Commission the named "Trust & Assurance" series.* This is the highest-ROI strategic move available to any publisher in 2026.

**Niche publishers (No Starch, Pragmatic):**
- No Starch should *own the offensive-AI security category outright* — 6-8 anchor titles in 36 months. The window to anchor the lane is 2026-2027.
- Pragmatic should pursue the *AI-native software engineering* category as the natural successor to *The Pragmatic Programmer*. This positions them for the senior-engineering segment that O'Reilly is conceding to Stripe Press / Pearson-AW.

### 2.2 Cross-cutting moves
- **Build verified-author programs by 2027.** Trust signals beat marketing as AI-generated content saturates the market.
- **Build live-edition infrastructure.** Books that update against frameworks are the practitioner default by 2028-2030.
- **License book content as AI-tutor source data.** Creates a new revenue layer and protects against unauthorized scraping.
- **Acquire 2-3 Substack-rooted authors per year per major imprint.**
- **Invest in Arabic, Hindi, Korean, Japanese, German native commissioning** — not just translation. Native commissioning gets the local case studies and regulatory framings that translations miss.

### 2.3 Risk management
- **AI-generated content erodes the bottom of the catalog.** Prepare for 30-50% of beginner-tier revenue to evaporate by 2030. Don't fight; reposition.
- **Open-access mandates compress academic-monograph margins.** Compensate via trade-imprint companions, not by retreating from monographs.
- **Vendor-cookbook obsolescence** is now 18-24 months. Don't price like a 5-year shelf-life book.

## 3. Recommendations for Universities

### 3.1 Curriculum modernization (priority order)
1. **Deprecate** introductory ML courses anchored on pre-transformer textbooks. Adopt the next-generation foundational AI textbook (a key gap; see [`04` G1](./04-market-gaps.md)) as soon as it ships in 2027-2028.
2. **Add** a graduate course in *agentic systems engineering* — the discipline currently has no canonical course but the demand is enormous.
3. **Add** a graduate course in *AI evaluations* — methodologically novel, employer-relevant.
4. **Add** a graduate course in *mechanistic interpretability* (textbook gap; [`04` §6](./04-market-gaps.md)).
5. **Add** a *VLA / robotics-AI* course replacing classical-only robotics in robotics programs.
6. **Add** a graduate course in *PQC engineering* — particularly in EE/CS programs in security tracks. Anchor with [`05` #46](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md) (lattice cryptography) and [`05` #38–#49](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md) for practitioner depth.
7. **Replace** introductory cryptography courses' last third with PQC + cryptographic agility content ([`05` #39](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)).
8. **Add** a *programmable-cryptography* graduate course (ZK + FHE + MPC + threshold) — anchor with [`05` #62–#63](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md).
9. **Add** a graduate course in *fault-tolerant quantum computing* anchored on [`05` #1, #3, #10](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md).
10. **Add** an *AI law / EU AI Act* course in law schools and policy schools.
11. **Add** an *AI-public-sector engineering* track in MPA programs (gap discussed in [`04`](./04-market-gaps.md)).
12. **Add** an *AI assurance and audit* concentration in MS-IS / MIS programs.
13. **Add** a *blockchain / tokenization for finance* course in MBA programs anchored on [`05` #73–#78](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md).

### 3.2 Research publishing
- Encourage faculty to publish *F&T-style surveys* before full monographs — better scholarly impact per hour, better acquisition signal for academic presses.
- Build *workshop-to-monograph* pipelines with Springer / ACM Books / CUP for newly-formed sub-fields (mechanistic interpretability, agentic systems theory, AI assurance, PQC migration).
- Invest in *open monograph* infrastructure aligned with funder mandates.

### 3.3 Specific institutional plays
- **MIT, Stanford, CMU, Berkeley, ETH, EPFL, Cambridge, Oxford, Tsinghua, Tokyo:** anchor at least one generational textbook per institution per major sub-field through 2030.
- **MBZUAI, NUS, KAIST, IIT Bombay, IIT Delhi, IIIT Hyderabad:** commission Arabic, Korean, Hindi, Japanese-language anchor textbooks now — a 5-10 year first-mover advantage.
- **Smaller research universities:** anchor *country-specific* AI-law / sovereign-AI / public-sector reference works. Lower commissioning bar, durable citation gravity.

## 4. Recommendations for Governments

### 4.1 STEM publishing as policy
- Commission *national-language* AI-literacy reference works as part of national AI strategies. Even small countries can anchor a defensible reference text in their language.
- Fund *AI-and-public-administration* reference works through procurement of senior practitioners + open-access publishing.
- Fund *PQC migration* reference works for critical-infrastructure operators in regulated languages.

### 4.2 National AI literacy programs
- Distribute books-as-public-goods through institutional licensing (libraries, community colleges, workforce boards). The unit cost is small; the cohort effect is large.
- Translate the top-tier English AI-literacy texts into national languages within 12 months of release. Most countries do this in 24-36 today. The gap matters.

### 4.3 Quantum workforce development
- Combine *quantum-readiness CTO playbook* ([`05` #32](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) with *PQC migration playbook* ([`05` #38](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) and *PQC audit & compliance* ([`05` #49](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) as a distributed government-procurement bundle for critical-infrastructure operators.
- Fund quantum-engineering graduate textbooks targeted at non-physicist engineers; the talent bottleneck is at the engineering-meets-physics seam.

### 4.4 Specific government plays
- **United States:** Federal AI use inventories require an operational reference work. Commission via GSA / DAU / NIST partnership.
- **EU:** Multi-language EU AI Act reference works are mandatory infrastructure. Fund via Horizon Europe.
- **UK:** AI Safety Institute should sponsor at least one generational reference work on frontier-model safety evaluations.
- **UAE / KSA:** Sovereign-language AI reference works as part of Vision 2030 / G42 / SDAIA programs.
- **India:** Digital-public-infrastructure (DPI) reference work is the single most globally citable book India can commission now.
- **Singapore / Hong Kong:** MAS Veritas / HKMA AI risk-management reference works for regional adoption.
- **Brazil:** Portuguese-language AI-finance reference work anchored on Pix and DREX cases.
- **Estonia:** *The eEstonia stack reference* — the most globally-citable digital-government book is uncommissioned.

## 5. Recommendations for Enterprises

### 5.1 Internal technical education programs
- **Books-as-curriculum** outperforms ad-hoc course consumption for senior-practitioner skill-building. Build cohort book-club programs around the BCQ senior-practitioner playbooks: PQC migration ([`05` #38](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)), cryptographic agility ([`05` #39](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)), smart-contract security ([`05` #64](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)), and the quantum-readiness executive set ([`05` #32–#37](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)). Senior AI playbooks (agentic systems, inference economics, AI-native engineering) are equally relevant though not numbered in the BCQ-focused Phase 5.
- **Cert-prep programs** combining ISACA AAIA + IAPP AIGP + ISO 42001 lead-auditor + the emerging PQC migration certs ([`05` #49](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md)) are the single highest-ROI internal training investment for any organization with an AI-or-crypto compliance function.
- **Smart-contract audit capability building** anchored on [`05` #64–#65, #88–#90](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md) for any organization holding tokenized assets, custody operations, or DeFi exposure.

### 5.2 Workforce upskilling publishing
- Co-publish industry-specific reference works with publishers under enterprise sponsorship. The case-study density of industry-anchored books is dramatically higher than independently-commissioned ones, and the publisher gets durable IP.
- **License books-as-AI-tutor-sources** for internal AI tutors trained on company data + curated reference texts.

### 5.3 AI transformation learning ecosystems
- Invest in *long-shelf-life* trade books over short-shelf-life vendor courses. Vendor courses become obsolete in 18 months; senior-engineering books survive 5-7 years.
- Use book + course + community + cert as the dominant L&D unit. The book is the *durable* layer.

## 6. Advanced Extensions (cross-cutting structural forces)

### 6.1 ISBN market trends
- ISBN allocations in technical-trade categories continue rising globally, driven by self-publishing.
- **Self-pub ISBN volume now exceeds trade ISBN volume** in some technical sub-categories (beginner Python, intro AI, intro crypto). The ISBN distribution alone is a leading indicator of category saturation.
- Publishers should *track ISBN allocation trends per topic* as a saturation early-warning system.

### 6.2 Amazon technical category analysis
- Amazon's "Computers & Technology > AI & Machine Learning" hovers among Amazon's fastest-growing book categories. Within it:
  - "AI Programming" — saturated.
  - "Natural Language Processing" — growth, more advanced titles needed.
  - "Generative AI" — saturated at intro tier.
  - "AI Ethics / AI Law" — under-supplied.
- Amazon's *bestseller-list dynamics* are now AI-mediated (recommendation engines + AI-generated review summaries). Books optimized for AI-mediated discoverability outperform.
- **Amazon's "category leakage"** — books appearing in categories the author did not pick — is a measurable signal that publishers should mine for cross-category opportunity.

### 6.3 Google Books and indexed-search trends
- Google Books indexing remains the dominant *citation-graph attractor* for academic monographs. Books not Google-Books-indexed receive ~10x fewer Google Scholar citations.
- Google's AI Overviews now cite books inline. Books with *clear definitional chapters* (early in the text) get cited disproportionately. Editors should prioritize crisp definitional structure.

### 6.4 Research-paper-to-book conversion
- The optimal conversion path is: paper → workshop volume → F&T survey → graduate textbook → trade-imprint companion.
- Publishers under-invest in *systematic* paper-to-book conversion. A publisher that builds an in-house "paper scout" function — tracking arXiv, NeurIPS, ICML, USENIX Security, S&P, Crypto, Eurocrypt for monograph candidates — gains a durable acquisition advantage.

### 6.5 AI-generated publishing risks
- The risk is *not* that AI-generated books replace expert-authored ones in senior segments — it is that they *flood the discovery surface* and erode signal.
- Mitigation: verified-author credentials, transparent AI-disclosure, editorial-process attestation, fact-check provenance.
- The legal risk (training-data IP, hallucinated facts, defamatory content) is non-trivial. Publishers should adopt the editorial equivalent of SBOMs ("manuscript bills of materials") by 2027.

### 6.6 Open-access publishing impact
- Plan-S-aligned mandates compress academic-book margins.
- Workaround patterns: trade-imprint companions, course-platform licensing, cert-prep adjacency, community editions with premium derivatives.
- The *companion structure* — an open-access primary monograph with a paid trade companion — becomes increasingly common.

### 6.7 Subscription learning ecosystem trends
- O'Reilly Online Learning, Pluralsight, Manning liveBook, Pearson Mastering, ACM/IEEE digital libraries — the subscription layer is the *primary* discovery surface for working engineers.
- Books optimized for *subscription consumption* (modular chapters, executable examples, embedded video) earn disproportionate engagement.
- Publishers without a subscription layer face pressure to either build one, partner, or specialize toward executive/cert formats not subscription-consumed.

### 6.8 Technical video learning competition analysis
- YouTube + Coursera + Udemy + Pluralsight collectively dominate *introductory* technical learning. Print/ebook is non-competitive in that segment.
- *Senior-engineering* and *certification* segments are the durable book strongholds.
- Best play: book + companion video, never video-replaces-book or book-replaces-video.

### 6.9 GitHub ecosystem influence on publishing demand
- **GitHub stars are a leading indicator of publishing demand.** Frameworks crossing ~10k stars consistently produce trade-publishable demand within 12-18 months.
- *Repos with `awesome-` prefixes and `cookbook` suffixes* are explicit signals of unmet documentation demand — publishers should monitor these as commissioning prompts.
- The *GitHub Issues / Discussions of major repos* are an under-used demand-signal source for publishers.

### 6.10 Developer community trend analysis
- Hacker News, Lobsters, Reddit r/MachineLearning, Hugging Face spaces, Discord servers (Latent Space, ML-Collective) — community-discussion volume per topic is a strong leading indicator of book demand 6-18 months out.
- Conference attendance (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, USENIX, Black Hat, DEF CON, KubeCon, Devcon, Devconnect, Q2B) tracks the leading edge of demand.
- Newsletter readership (Substack newsletters with >5k engaged readers in a focused niche) is the strongest *individual-author leading indicator* of book demand.

### 6.11 Certification economy impact
- The cert economy is the **highest-margin durable revenue layer** in technical publishing.
- Each established cert supports 3-7 prep-book versions across publishers, evergreen 5-10 years, with workbook + flashcard + practice-test spinoffs.
- Best plays: ISO 42001, ISACA AAIA, IAPP AIGP, NIST AI RMF practitioner, CMMC + AI, MAS Veritas, PQC migration cert (forming).

### 6.12 Enterprise AI upskilling demand forecasting
- Internal enterprise spend on AI-engineering upskilling is projected to grow at >20% CAGR through 2030 (rough triangulation across enterprise L&D research; not a precise forecast).
- *Senior-engineering books licensed to internal libraries* are a durable revenue layer largely unexploited by trade publishers. The licensing infrastructure exists (CCC, Cambridge Online, ProQuest); deal-flow does not yet match demand.
- *Books-as-AI-tutor-source* licensing — where books are licensed as ground truth for an enterprise's internal AI tutor — is a 2027-onward revenue layer publishers should build product for now.

## 7. The five plays that matter most

If a publishing organization or author can take only one or two of the following over the next 24 months, these are the plays:

1. **Commission a "Trust & Assurance" series.** AI assurance + PQC migration + cert-prep, 10-12 anchor titles, 5-language editions, 36-month rollout. The single highest-ROI program available.
2. **Anchor a generational graduate textbook in 2026 for 2028 publication.** The successor to *Deep Learning*, or the first VLA robotics textbook, or the first mechanistic-interpretability textbook. 10-year curriculum dominance follows.
3. **Build an "AI-native engineering" senior-practitioner line.** 4-6 opinionated books for the post-tutorial era. Highest brand-equity program.
4. **Acquire / commission three Substack-rooted authors.** Each is worth more than a typical mid-list book. Build the relationship before competitors do.
5. **Native multi-language commissioning** in Arabic, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, German. Not translation — native authoring with regional case studies.

A publisher executing all five wins the next decade. An author choosing to write the right title under the right house with the right format does the same on the individual scale.
