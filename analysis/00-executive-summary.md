# Executive Summary

> *The 12 things that matter, for a publisher exec or acquisitions editor in a 10-minute read.*

This is a global technical book publication catalog analysis covering Blockchain, AI, Quantum, Web3, AI-cybersecurity, AI infrastructure, quantum cryptography, edge AI, decentralized systems, and emerging deep tech — across the United States (federal + 50 states clustered), Europe (20+ countries), and major global hubs (Canada, Israel, UAE, KSA, India, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, China, Australia, LATAM, Africa).

The full report is in seven phases (`01`-`07`). This is the strategic distillation.

---

## The 12 findings that shape the next five years

### 1 — The single highest-ROI commissioning slot in trade publishing today is *PQC migration*.
The math is closed: NIST has finalized FIPS 203/204/205 (with HQC selected as the additional KEM, March 2025); CNSA 2.0 / NSM-10 mandate enterprise migration; EU, UK, German BSI, French ANSSI, Australian ASD, Japanese NICT equivalents follow; and *no comprehensive practitioner handbook exists*. A publisher who ships *The Post-Quantum Migration Playbook* in 2026 owns a 5-7 year evergreen with mandated buyers. (See [`05` #38](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md), [`04` G2](./04-market-gaps.md).)

### 2 — *AI assurance / governance / audit* is structurally orphaned.
No publisher owns it the way O'Reilly owns LLM apps or No Starch owns offensive security. EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, ISACA AAIA, and IAPP AIGP collectively create a guaranteed buyer cohort of compliance, audit, GRC, and model-risk professionals. The publisher who launches a *named* AI Assurance series in 2026-2027 owns the lane through 2030. (See [`02` matrix](./02-publisher-catalogs.md), [`04` Tier 1](./04-market-gaps.md).)

### 3 — The next *generational* deep-learning textbook is overdue and uncommissioned.
*Deep Learning* (Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville, 2016) remains the canonical graduate text but is increasingly out of step with the transformer/agentic/multimodal era. The first press to anchor a true successor — MIT, CUP, or Cambridge — captures a decade of curriculum dominance. (Discussed in [`04` G1](./04-market-gaps.md); not numbered in the BCQ-focused [`05`](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md).)

### 4 — *Agentic systems engineering* is graduating from "framework collection" to "engineering discipline."
The framework-of-the-month problem makes most beginner agentic books obsolete in 12-18 months. The right play is a *senior-engineering* book on agent architecture as a discipline (state, memory, tool use, evaluation, supervision, observability) that survives churn — paired with rapid-update vendor cookbooks. (Discussed in [`03` §1.3](./03-technology-trends.md); not numbered in the BCQ-focused [`05`](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md).)

### 5 — Quantum trade publishing is mis-pitched.
The Qiskit/Cirq beginner shelf is over-supplied; cloud vendors give those tutorials away. The under-supplied tier is *quantum-readiness* for CTOs and CISOs, plus PQC migration. The commercial-quantum book that sells in 2026-2030 is not a quantum-programming tutorial — it is an enterprise readiness playbook. (See [`03` §3](./03-technology-trends.md), [`05` #32](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md).)

### 6 — *AI for quantum control* (not QML) is the real AI × quantum intersection.
QML hype is deflating. The genuine and growing intersection is RL for pulse shaping, ML for QEC decoders, ML for calibration. Book-lane is empty. The first F&T-style monograph anchors a citation graph for years. (See [`03` §4.3](./03-technology-trends.md), [`05` #94](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md).)

### 7 — *Tokenization of real-world assets* is the dominant institutional-blockchain story of 2026-2030.
BlackRock-tier institutional flows; tokenized treasuries, money-market funds, syndicated credit; mandatory custody and compliance reference work demand from banks, asset managers, fintechs. The cross-disciplinary RWA reference book is the largest unfilled slot in finance-tech publishing. (See [`03` §2.4](./03-technology-trends.md), [`05` #73](./05-top-100-book-ideas.md).)

### 8 — Regulation is now the largest single demand generator for technical publishing.
EU AI Act, NIS2, NIST AI RMF, MAS Veritas, eSafety, Korea AI Basic Act, Japan AI Promotion Act, NSM-10/CNSA 2.0, eIDAS 2.0/EUDI Wallet — each creates a *guaranteed* buyer cohort with hardback-priced willingness-to-pay. The 2026-2030 cycle is the single most regulation-aligned publishing cycle in the field's history. ([`01` §1](./01-global-landscape.md), [`06` §2](./06-forecasts.md).)

### 9 — English-language hegemony is plateauing; sovereign-language commissioning is the quiet land grab.
EU AI Act forces native German / French / Italian / Spanish; Vision 2030 + G42 + MBZUAI demand Arabic; Japan, Korea, India demand sovereign-language commissioning. The 2026-2030 cycle rebalances toward regional editions *in addition to* English. Packt and Apress are best structurally positioned; native-commissioning beats translation. ([`01` §5](./01-global-landscape.md), [`04` §5](./04-market-gaps.md).)

### 10 — The "Tokyo–Delhi–Tel-Aviv–Boston monograph corridor" is where the highest-citation deep-tech monographs originate.
Acquisitions calendars should treat these four cities as a single shopping map for advanced AI / quantum / cryptography monographs. Add Cambridge (UK), Zürich, Delft, Munich, Beijing, Toronto, and Edmonton for a complete monograph-source map. ([`01` §5](./01-global-landscape.md).)

### 11 — Self-publishing has captured 30-40% of *beginner* AI/Web3 titles and will reach 60-70% by 2030.
Trade publishers should *cede* the beginner shelf to self-pub and AI generation, and concentrate on senior-practitioner playbooks, academic monographs, executive briefings, and certification preparation — the four tiers where editorial value, trust signals, and curation justify price premium. ([`06` §8](./06-forecasts.md), [`07` §2](./07-strategic-recommendations.md).)

### 12 — AI-generated technical content reshapes the medium, not the value proposition.
By 2027-2028, AI assists ~70-90% of practitioner-book drafting in mainstream trade. Editorial work shifts toward *scoping, sourcing, narrative coherence, fact-checking* — not prose generation. Verified-author programs, AI-disclosure standards, manuscript-bills-of-materials, and live editions become standard infrastructure. The risk is signal erosion at the bottom of the catalog; the opportunity is faster, broader, multi-language coverage at the top. ([`06` §7](./06-forecasts.md).)

## The five plays that matter most

If you can take only one or two of these over the next 24 months:

1. **Commission a "Trust & Assurance" series.** AI assurance + PQC migration + cert-prep companions. 10-12 anchor titles, 5-language editions, 36-month rollout. *Highest-ROI program of the decade.*
2. **Anchor a generational graduate textbook in 2026 for 2028 publication.** Successor to *Deep Learning*, or first VLA robotics textbook, or first mechanistic-interpretability textbook. *10-year curriculum dominance follows.*
3. **Launch an "AI-native engineering" senior-practitioner line.** 4-6 opinionated books for the post-tutorial era. *Highest brand-equity program.*
4. **Acquire / commission three Substack-rooted authors per year.** *Highest individual-asset return.*
5. **Native multi-language commissioning** in Arabic, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, German. Native authoring with regional case studies — not translation. *Lowest-risk geographic expansion.*

## Methodology and limitations

This report is a strategist's synthesis, not a survey. Findings are built from publicly observable patterns in publisher catalogs, university curricula, research funding flows, conference proceedings, regulatory developments, and developer-community signals through mid-2026.

Numerical scores (COS, sub-axes, archetype rankings) are *relative ordinal estimates* — useful as a comparative lens, not as audited statistics. Where regulatory deadlines and standardization milestones anchor a forecast (PQC, AI Act), confidence is high; where progression depends on foundation-model capability advances, confidence degrades with horizon.

The point of the exercise is not to be precisely right about every score; it is to provide a *defensible decision frame* for which titles to commission, which to skip, and which programs to anchor.
