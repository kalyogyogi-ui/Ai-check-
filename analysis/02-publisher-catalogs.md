# Phase 2 — Publisher Catalog Analysis

> *What does each major publisher already own, where are their catalogs structurally thin, and which 2026-2030 wave is each best positioned to capture?*

A publisher's *catalog* is its strategic memory. Reading catalogs sideways across topics — and lengthwise across years — exposes who has *front-list velocity* (rapid new-title throughput) versus who has *back-list moat* (durable, citable references). The two strategies suit different waves: front-list velocity wins in early-growth tech (LLM tooling, agentic engineering); back-list moat wins in mature theory (quantum information, cryptography, ML foundations).

---

## 1. Commercial Trade Publishers

### O'Reilly Media — `velocity 10 / moat 7`
**Catalog gravity:** AI/ML practitioner reference, cloud, data engineering. *The* default for working software/data engineers in the West.
**Strengths:** "Animal book" brand equity; first-mover on every major AI wave (TF, PyTorch, LangChain, agentic). Live-online platform converts books into curriculum. Strong "X with Python" series economics.
**Gaps:**
- Deep AI theory and graduate textbooks (cedes to MIT/Cambridge).
- Quantum computing — minimal catalog presence.
- Formal verification, AI assurance, model risk — present but thin.
- Non-English original commissioning is limited.
**Saturation by topic:**
- LLM application engineering — **SAT** (oversupplied)
- Agentic systems — **GR** (still room for senior practitioner takes)
- AI infra (training, inference, MLOps) — **GR** (under-supplied at advanced tier)
- Blockchain — **DEC** (catalog has thinned post-2022)
- Quantum — **EM** (greenfield)
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** AI-infra at scale, eval/safety engineering, agentic-systems senior playbooks, AI-data-platforms, AI-DevOps, AI-finance practitioner reference.

### Manning Publications — `velocity 9 / moat 6`
**Catalog gravity:** "...in Action" practitioner books; MEAP early-access model captures pre-launch signal.
**Strengths:** Fast on emerging topics (often 2-4 months ahead of O'Reilly on niche frameworks). Strong Java/Kotlin/.NET hold; growing AI/ML in Action line. liveBook + liveProject pipeline.
**Gaps:** Academic credibility ceiling; rarely adopted as primary university texts. Quantum essentially absent. Limited non-English commissioning.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** Mid-level practitioner agentic engineering, RAG-in-action, LangChain/LangGraph/LlamaIndex deep-dives, on-device LLMs, Solana/EVM-in-action revivals, robotics-AI in action.

### Packt Publishing — `velocity 10 / moat 4`
**Catalog gravity:** Highest *front-list throughput* in tech publishing; massive India-anchored production engine.
**Strengths:** Speed, breadth, niche coverage no other publisher will commission, B2B subscription (Packt+) and library deals. Particularly strong in cookbook-style and "with X cloud" formats.
**Gaps:** Per-title editorial depth varies; back-list durability is short (many titles obsolete in 2-3 years). Academic adoption is rare. Brand premium is constrained.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** Cloud-AI-vendor-specific cookbooks (Bedrock, Vertex, Azure-AI), Solidity/Foundry/Hardhat practical, ZK-circuit cookbooks, agentic-framework cookbooks, AI-on-edge-devices cookbooks. *Volume-by-niche* is the right strategy here, not flagship titles.

### Pragmatic Bookshelf — `velocity 6 / moat 7`
**Catalog gravity:** Opinionated practitioner books with high editorial polish; smaller but more durable than Packt.
**Strengths:** Strong Ruby/Elixir/Rust roots, growing ML and Phoenix/LiveView intersection, careful editing.
**Gaps:** Slow to enter AI; no quantum; no blockchain depth.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** Rust-AI (a structurally underserved combination), Elixir/Nx for ML serving, secure-by-design AI applications, opinionated agent-engineering primers.

### No Starch Press — `velocity 5 / moat 8`
**Catalog gravity:** Hacker/security/curiosity books; durable back-list.
**Strengths:** Deep cybersecurity, reverse engineering, low-level systems; growing AI-security adjacency.
**Gaps:** Mainstream AI engineering is not a fit; quantum is a stretch except for QKD/PQC-security framings.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** **AI red-teaming, prompt-injection attacks, model-extraction, ML-system exploitation, LLM-agent security, post-quantum cryptography for practitioners** — No Starch is structurally the best home for the entire offensive-AI-security category.

### Addison-Wesley / Pearson Professional — `velocity 5 / moat 9`
**Catalog gravity:** Classic CS canon (Knuth, Stevens, Bloch, Stroustrup). The reference shelf of the discipline.
**Strengths:** Series equity (e.g. *…Series* with named curators), durable adoption, strong typesetting.
**Gaps:** Slow to commission; risk-averse on speculative topics; academic-trade hybrid sometimes lands neither.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** New canonical references — *The Agentic Systems Programming Language* of the AI era, AI-systems classics in the SICP/CSAPP tradition, formal-methods-for-ML reference. The right play is *one* generational textbook, not a series sprint.

### Sams Publishing — `velocity 4 / moat 5`
Now largely a Pearson sub-brand for "Teach Yourself in 24 Hours" / "in X Days" formats. AI/blockchain/quantum coverage is limited and not strategically priority. Plays here are introductory, mass-market, certification-adjacent.

### Apress — `velocity 8 / moat 6`
**Catalog gravity:** Springer-owned trade tech imprint; strong .NET, cloud, mid-level AI.
**Strengths:** Bridges Springer academic distribution with practitioner-style writing. Often the best home for an "academic practitioner" title.
**Gaps:** Brand recognition with developers is below O'Reilly/Manning; quantum is light.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** Practitioner-meets-research books (e.g., "Agentic Systems with Theoretical Foundations"), enterprise-AI architecture books, blockchain-for-finance executive-engineer hybrids.

### McGraw-Hill (Professional/Education) — `velocity 5 / moat 8`
**Catalog gravity:** Education + certification. Schaum's Outlines tradition.
**Strengths:** Curriculum-aligned textbooks, certification press, durable educational distribution.
**Gaps:** Front-list velocity is low; AI-trade titles are sparse.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** AI literacy curricula, AI-for-business core textbooks, certification prep (CompTIA AI/Sec, AWS/Azure AI specialty), state-workforce-board institutional packages.

### Wiley (incl. Sybex, For Dummies) — `velocity 7 / moat 8`
**Catalog gravity:** Massive — academic + professional + trade. Sybex anchors certification.
**Strengths:** AI-finance, AI-actuarial, AI-healthcare via Wiley professional; Sybex for cloud/security certs; *For Dummies* for mass-market AI literacy.
**Gaps:** Editorial coherence across imprints is uneven; cutting-edge practitioner AI cedes to O'Reilly.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** AI in regulated industries (finance, insurance, pharma, healthcare) — Wiley's professional vertical is structurally well-positioned. Sybex-track AI/Sec certifications. *For Dummies* AI literacy mass-market.

## 2. Academic / University Presses

### Springer Nature — `velocity 8 / moat 10`
**Catalog gravity:** The largest STEM publisher worldwide; LNCS, LNAI, *Foundations and Trends* equivalents, *Encyclopedia of …* series, monographs in mathematics, physics, CS.
**Strengths:** Global academic distribution, conference-proceedings dominance (LNCS), monograph series velocity, strong quantum/cryptography catalog.
**Gaps:** Per-title pricing and DRM hurt practitioner adoption; trade visibility low.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** Quantum-information monographs (already strong, room to extend), agentic-AI graduate textbooks, AI-safety monograph series, lattice-cryptography reference works, LNAI agentic-systems proceedings stream.

### Wiley (Academic) — see Wiley above
Strong in engineering and applied AI textbooks; less strong in pure ML theory.

### Elsevier (academic books, distinct from journals) — `velocity 6 / moat 9`
**Strengths:** Strong in computational science, signal processing, controls, biomedical AI; Morgan Kaufmann imprint anchors classic AI/data-mining texts.
**Gaps:** Reputational headwinds in OA debates; slow to embrace open monographs.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** Morgan Kaufmann revival — agentic systems, data-centric AI, AI-for-engineering-design. Biomedical AI textbooks and AI-imaging are natural lanes.

### MIT Press — `velocity 6 / moat 10`
**Catalog gravity:** *The* English-language anchor for foundational AI and computational thinking (Goodfellow et al.; Sutton & Barto; the Marr/Minsky lineage).
**Strengths:** Editorial prestige, durable adoption, growing AI-policy and AI-ethics list (Strong Ideas series).
**Gaps:** Throughput is intentionally low; risk-averse on practitioner formats.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** The *next-generation* foundational AI textbooks — there is room for a successor to *Deep Learning* (2016) tuned to the transformer/agentic era. AI-policy reference works. Quantum-information graduate textbook revival.

### Cambridge University Press — `velocity 7 / moat 10`
**Catalog gravity:** Strong in cryptography, statistical learning, algorithmic game theory, philosophy of AI.
**Strengths:** Global English-language distribution; excellent in ML theory and PQC mathematics.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** PQC mathematics + standardization companion works, algorithmic information theory updates, AI-policy academic monographs (CSER-adjacent), graduate texts on alignment / model evaluations.

### Oxford University Press — `velocity 7 / moat 10`
**Catalog gravity:** Strong in AI ethics, philosophy of AI, law and AI, computational social science.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** AI law (especially EU AI Act + UK AISI-aligned), AI and political economy, AI in international relations, philosophy of agency. *The* home for the deep-policy reference shelf.

### CRC Press / Chapman & Hall — `velocity 7 / moat 8`
**Catalog gravity:** Engineering reference, applied math, statistics, security, signal processing.
**Best 2026-2030 plays:** AI for control systems, signal-processing-meets-ML reference, applied cryptography handbooks, AI in medical imaging, quantum signal processing.

### Birkhäuser (Springer) — `velocity 5 / moat 9`
Mathematical depth; quantum information mathematics, applied harmonic analysis intersecting ML, optimization theory. Niche but durable.

### Princeton University Press — `velocity 4 / moat 9`
Strong in AI ethics, mathematical reference (Princeton Companions), computational social science. Best plays: AI-and-society reference works, mathematical-AI references in the *Companion* tradition.

### Yale University Press — `velocity 4 / moat 8`
Strong in AI policy, technology and democracy, surveillance studies. Best plays: AI-democracy, AI-and-the-public-sphere, AI-misinformation.

### IEEE Press / ACM Books — `velocity 5 / moat 9`
Society publishing; under-monetized but uniquely *citable*. Best plays: IEEE-anchored AI engineering standards companion books, ACM Books series on agentic systems, IEEE PQC migration handbooks.

### Now Publishers (*Foundations and Trends*) — `velocity 5 / moat 9`
The premier *survey monograph* press. Best plays: agentic AI surveys, model-evaluation surveys, quantum-ML surveys — uniquely sized between paper and book.

## 3. Self-Published / Independent Ecosystems

### Leanpub
- Live early-access, royalty-favorable. Captures *pre-Manning-MEAP* signal on emerging frameworks.
- Currently the *first* venue for many agentic-engineering, MLX, ZK-tooling, and Solidity-Foundry titles.

### GitBook / mdBook / Docusaurus-as-book
- Open-source-adjacent books. The Bitcoin and Ethereum communities have effectively replaced trade publishers for protocol documentation.

### Substack-to-book pipeline
- Increasingly common: a successful technical newsletter (~5-50k subscribers) becomes a self-published book that out-sells trade equivalents in the first year. Trade publishers are now competing for *Substack rights* the way they once competed for blog rights.

### Amazon KDP technical
- Mid-tier practitioner books, especially in non-English (Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi, Arabic). Quality variance is high but the market is *real*.

### Implication for trade publishers
- The "first 100k words on a new framework" niche has migrated to self-publishing.
- Trade publishers should *acquire from* this ecosystem (commission revisions of high-signal Leanpub/GitBook titles) rather than try to *outrun* it.

## 5. Publisher-level SWOT

### O'Reilly — SWOT
- **S:** Brand, platform, animal-book equity, instructor network.
- **W:** Beginner glut crowds out advanced; quantum gap; non-English thin.
- **O:** AI infra, AI eval, post-AGI engineering practices, "AI for X-industry" expansions.
- **T:** Self-publishing erosion of beginner segment; AI-generated tutorial content.

### Manning — SWOT
- **S:** MEAP signal, fast turnaround, Java/.NET legacy.
- **W:** Less academic credibility; uneven editing on outer-niche titles.
- **O:** Agentic engineering practitioner segment, robotics-AI, mobile-LLM.
- **T:** Squeezed between Packt (volume) and O'Reilly (brand).

### Packt — SWOT
- **S:** Volume, India production, niche coverage no one else attempts.
- **W:** Back-list decay, brand premium ceiling.
- **O:** Multi-language editions (own the Indian + LATAM + Arabic practitioner market), AI-vendor cookbook series.
- **T:** AI-generated technical content directly competes with Packt's mid-tier output.

### Springer — SWOT
- **S:** Distribution, monograph series, conference proceedings.
- **W:** Pricing/DRM friction; trade invisibility.
- **O:** PQC + quantum-info dominance; AI-safety monograph series; agentic AI graduate textbook lane.
- **T:** Open-access mandates compress traditional monograph margins.

### MIT Press — SWOT
- **S:** Editorial prestige, generational textbooks, Strong Ideas.
- **W:** Low throughput.
- **O:** Successor to *Deep Learning*; foundational agentic-AI textbook; AI-policy canonical reference shelf.
- **T:** First-mover in canonical agentic-AI textbook is contested by CUP/OUP.

### Wiley — SWOT
- **S:** Regulated-industry access, cert, mass-market.
- **W:** Imprint coherence; cutting-edge AI cedes.
- **O:** AI in finance/healthcare/insurance/pharma; AI-cert via Sybex; *For Dummies* AI literacy at global scale.
- **T:** Industry-association-direct publishing routes around Wiley's vertical model.

### CRC / Taylor & Francis — SWOT
- **S:** Engineering, controls, signal processing, applied math.
- **W:** Less prestige in pure CS theory.
- **O:** Industrial AI, AI in manufacturing, AI in physical systems.
- **T:** Engineering associations (ASME, IEEE) self-publishing reference works.

### CUP / OUP — SWOT
- **S:** Authority on policy, philosophy, theory.
- **W:** Slow throughput, less practitioner reach.
- **O:** AI law, AI and democracy, philosophy of agency, foundational graduate texts.
- **T:** Open-access pressure on policy monographs.

### No Starch — SWOT
- **S:** Hacker brand, security depth, durable back-list.
- **W:** Narrow tonal fit; not the natural home for mainstream AI engineering.
- **O:** **Own the entire AI/LLM offensive-security category** — prompt injection, agent exploitation, model extraction, multi-agent attack surfaces.
- **T:** AI-security niche briefly attracts every publisher; window to anchor is now.

### Apress — SWOT
- **S:** Springer distribution + practitioner tone.
- **W:** Brand recognition with developers.
- **O:** Academic-practitioner hybrid AI books; enterprise architecture books.
- **T:** Squeezed between Manning and Springer flagship.

### Pearson Professional — SWOT
- **S:** Series equity (Addison-Wesley canon), educational distribution.
- **W:** Slow commissioning, risk-averse.
- **O:** New canonical references in the AW tradition for AI-systems and agentic engineering.
- **T:** Late entry costs ground to MIT/CUP/Springer.

## 6. Strategic synthesis

1. **The commissioning map is now bimodal.** Frontier-velocity publishers (Packt, Manning, O'Reilly, Leanpub) compete for *years 0-2* of every wave; canonical publishers (MIT, CUP, OUP, Springer, Pearson-AW) compete for *years 3-15*. Hybrid publishers (Apress, Wiley, CRC) have to pick a lane per topic — they cannot win both.
2. **The AI-eval and AI-assurance category is structurally orphaned.** No publisher owns it the way O'Reilly owned LLM-app dev or No Starch owns offensive security. The first to commission a *named series* (e.g., "AI Assurance Engineering") with 6-8 anchor titles will own this lane through 2030.
3. **The PQC operational-migration book is the highest-COS unfilled slot in trade publishing today.** Math is well-covered; standards are stabilizing; enterprises are mandated to migrate; nobody has the practitioner bridge book. Whoever ships first wins a 5-7 year evergreen.
4. **Quantum computing trade publishing is still mis-pitched.** Most "quantum for engineers" books target software developers who do not need to act yet. The right *commercial* quantum books for 2026-2028 are *quantum-readiness for CTOs* and *PQC-migration for security teams* — not Qiskit tutorials, which the cloud vendors give away.
5. **Sovereign-language commissioning (Arabic, Hindi, Korean, Japanese, German) is the quiet land grab.** Packt and Apress are best structurally positioned to convert anchor English titles into adapted regional editions at scale.
