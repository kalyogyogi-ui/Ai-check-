# Phase 6 — Future Forecasting

> *Five horizons: 2026, 2027, 2028, 2030, 2035. What does the technical publishing industry look like at each, and how does AI itself reshape the medium?*

Forecasts here use a **Gartner-style three-band structure**: a *base case* (most likely), a *fast case* (acceleration), and a *brake case* (regulatory or financial drag). Confidence is highest at 18-month horizons and degrades roughly linearly to 2035.

---

## 1. Horizon 2026 (now → end of year)

**Confidence: high.** Most of 2026 is already shaped by commitments made in 2024-2025.

### What happens
- **EU AI Act** core obligations bind for general-purpose AI providers and high-risk systems. Compliance handbook demand spikes hard, especially in DE / FR / IT / ES.
- **NIST AI 600-1 (GenAI Profile)** + ISO/IEC 42001 + ISACA AAIA + IAPP AIGP form a *de facto* AI-assurance certification stack. The cert-prep book wave begins.
- **NSM-10 / CNSA 2.0 milestones** push large US federal contractors into PQC inventory and pilot phase. Practitioner PQC books ship into a guaranteed-buyer market.
- **Agentic engineering** consolidates from "framework collection" to "engineering discipline." Senior-practitioner book lane opens.
- **On-device LLMs** (Apple Foundation Models class, Snapdragon-X NPU class, Gemini Nano class) cross from demo to default. Edge-AI book demand inflects.
- **Self-publishing share of beginner technical content** crosses ~40% in AI/Web3 niches.
- **AI-generated draft books** appear in volume on Amazon KDP and at the bottom of Packt's outer-niche stack. The market begins distinguishing *editorially-curated* from *machine-generated* technical content as a quality signal.

### Publishing read
- **Buy:** AI-assurance, PQC migration, EU-AI-Act-in-practice, edge-LLM practitioner, agentic systems senior playbooks.
- **Sell / discontinue:** generic LLM-introduction backlist, beginner blockchain, beginner Solidity, Qiskit-tutorial introductions.
- **Pause:** decentralized-AI / on-chain-inference (let the speculative cycle clear).

### Risk asymmetries
- Commissioning a generic *Build with ChatGPT* title in 2026 has near-zero upside. Commissioning *PQC Migration Playbook* in 2026 has near-zero downside.

## 2. Horizon 2027

**Confidence: high.**

### What happens
- **EU AI Act** full application (general-purpose + high-risk + sandboxes) drives an avalanche of corrective second editions and new commissions.
- **First wave of "AI-native" enterprise re-orgs** completes; org-design and capability books targeted at executives sell at scale.
- **Quantum hardware milestones** (1000+ logical qubits at multi-vendor scale, neutral-atom and superconducting parity) shift quantum-strategy books from speculative to credible. *Quantum-readiness CTO playbook* category becomes mass-market.
- **Federal AI assurance procurement clauses** (US, UK, EU) become standard. Government-AI-procurement reference books mandatory.
- **Self-published Substack-rooted authors** become the dominant source of the highest-citation practitioner texts. Trade publishers acquire 2-3 such manuscripts each per year.
- **AI-native code editors** (Cursor / Windsurf / Copilot Workspace / Claude Code-class) become default in mid-tier engineering. *Engineering-with-AI* books outsell most introductory programming books.
- **Korean AI Basic Act, Japanese AI Promotion Act, Canadian AIDA** bind in some form. Country-specific AI-law shelves expand.

### Publishing read
- **Buy:** comparative AI law, EU AI Act second editions, AI-procurement, sovereign-AI strategy, AI-native software engineering, AgentOps.
- **Sell / discontinue:** introductory MLOps (subsumed by AgentOps and AI-platform engineering), generic data-science introductions.
- **Pause:** Quantum ML monographs (let the deflation finish).

### Inflection point
- The **AI-assurance certification cohort** crosses ~100k cumulative cert holders globally in 2027. The cert-prep market is now self-sustaining.

---

## 3. Horizon 2028

**Confidence: medium-high.**

### What happens
- **First "post-canonical" AI textbook** establishes its position in graduate curricula (most likely successor to *Deep Learning*, anchored on transformer + agentic + multimodal foundations).
- **Mechanistic interpretability** crystallizes into a teachable graduate subject. First textbook anchors course adoption.
- **Tokenization of regulated assets** (treasury bills, money-market funds, syndicated credit, private equity wrappers) is *normalized* in major banking centers. Reference-book demand from audit, custody, treasury, and risk teams remains durable.
- **Quantum networking pilots** at metropolitan scale (US, EU, China, Japan) become operational. Quantum-internet textbook lane opens to course adoption.
- **Crypto-agility** is a procurement requirement, not a stretch goal. Books on cryptographic-agility *engineering* outsell most general-cryptography titles.
- **Robotics × VLA** crosses a commercial threshold (humanoid + warehouse + automotive) and a *Russ-Tedrake-successor*-class textbook anchors curriculum globally.
- **AI publishing platforms with verified-author and verified-editor credentialing** emerge as a quality-signal layer; major trade publishers join or co-found.
- **Open-access mandates** (Plan S successor, NIH Public Access, EU Horizon mandates) compress academic-monograph margins. Academic publishers compensate with *companion* practitioner trade titles per monograph.

### Publishing read
- **Buy:** mechanistic-interpretability textbook, VLA robotics textbook, quantum-networking textbook, RWA tokenization second editions, AI-native software engineering second editions.
- **Sell / discontinue:** older deep-learning textbooks (durably superseded), generic agentic-framework cookbooks pegged to 2024-2025 frameworks.

## 4. Horizon 2030

**Confidence: medium.**

### Three scenarios

**Base case.** Foundation-model capabilities continue improving at *deflationary cost*. Practitioner skills shift from *implementation* to *integration, evaluation, and governance.* Book demand moves up the abstraction stack: fewer "how to use library X" books, more "how to design and govern systems that use libraries." Total industry revenue is flat-to-up; volume is flat-to-down; price-per-unit rises (more hardback monographs, fewer cheap paperbacks). The certification economy is the dominant durable revenue layer.

**Fast case.** AGI-adjacent agent systems begin replacing *most* tutorial-grade content. The technical-trade market bifurcates: (1) highly-edited senior-engineering trade books with verified-expert provenance — *premium pricing, smaller volume*; (2) AI-generated tutorial firehoses subsidized by cloud vendors — *zero or near-zero pricing, infinite volume*. Mid-tier publishers under pressure.

**Brake case.** Regulatory and energy constraints slow foundation-model progress. The 2027-2028 LLM book wave becomes durable; current "speculative" topics (decentralized AI, agentic AI) deflate. Quantum and PQC slow modestly. The publishing industry looks more like 2022 with PQC mandated.

### Trends across all scenarios
- **English-language hegemony erodes from 75-80% to 60-65%** of technical-publishing revenue. Arabic, Hindi, Spanish, Korean, Japanese, German rise.
- **Audio + video + interactive companions** are the default; text-only is the exception. Most major titles ship with companion artifacts (notebook, interactive course, AI tutor, video).
- **Live, model-aware editions** of practitioner books — books that update against an AI tutor and refresh examples — emerge from O'Reilly Live, Manning liveBook, Pearson Mastering, and a wave of new AI-native book platforms.
- **The publisher-as-platform** model (Stripe Press, Pragmatic Bookshelf, more) gains share against legacy trade publishers in the senior-engineering segment.
- **Universities license book pipelines, not individual titles.** Institutional licensing shifts toward all-you-can-read at the campus level.

## 5. Horizon 2035

**Confidence: low (directional only).**

A 9-year horizon is past the point of credible numbers. We can describe *structural transitions* with reasonable confidence and decline to estimate magnitudes.

### Structural transitions likely by 2035
- **The "book" as a unit of technical knowledge transmission decouples from the printed object** but does not disappear. Books remain *the canonical reference object* for human-curated knowledge, while AI tutors and live courses are the dominant consumption surface.
- **Provenance and attribution are first-class metadata.** Every technical book carries verifiable expert attestation and dataset provenance, in part because regulators require it and in part because the market demands it as a quality signal.
- **The AI/quantum publishing distinction blurs.** Books increasingly cover *AI-and-quantum-together* (post-quantum AI infrastructure, quantum-augmented agentic systems, and so on).
- **Open-access becomes the academic norm.** Trade-imprint companions are the dominant monetization path for academic publishers.
- **A *generational textbook* generation is now the canonical anchor of curricula** — most CS-AI graduates enter the workforce having learned from textbooks commissioned in 2026-2028.
- **Publishing economics: decoupling.** Trade publishers monetize by *trust + curation + community*, not by typesetting and distribution. Self-publishing infrastructure (Leanpub-class) does the operational labor, while publishers add editorial brand and credentialing.
- **Quantum cryptography is no longer a separate book shelf.** It has fully merged into the cryptography canon, and post-classical assumptions are baseline.
- **Robotics-AI is a mass-market trade category** rivaling AI-application books in 2026.

## 7. AI's impact on technical publishing itself

### 7.1 Production
- **Drafting:** AI assists ~70-90% of practitioner-book drafting by 2027-2028 in mainstream trade. Editorial work shifts toward *scoping, sourcing, narrative coherence, fact-checking* — not prose generation.
- **Translation:** AI translates ~95% of technical-book translations by 2027 (German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Hindi, Arabic). Localized practitioner books ship 6-12 months after the English edition by default — vs 18-36 today.
- **Updates:** *Live editions* (the book updates against current frameworks) become the default for fast-moving practitioner segments. The "static printed text + online errata" model degrades.

### 7.2 Distribution
- **Search and discovery shift to AI-mediated retrieval.** Buyers ask an AI tutor "what is the best book on X?" — and the answer determines purchase. Publishers optimize *for AI-system citation*, similar to how they once optimized for SEO.
- **Personalized table-of-contents** — buyers receive a TOC tuned to their level. Static one-size-fits-all editions decline; modular catalogs rise.
- **Bundling rises.** Book + course + cert + AI tutor + community is the dominant SKU by 2030.

### 7.3 Authorship and trust
- **Verified-author credentials become a market signal.** Major publishers introduce attestation programs by 2027-2028 ("this book was written by a person, fact-checked by a person, with disclosed AI assistance percentages").
- **Self-publishing market splits** between AI-generated tutorial firehoses (zero-trust, near-zero price) and verified-expert self-published premium titles (high trust, high price). The middle compresses.

### 7.4 Risks
- **AI-generated technical books at scale erode beginner-tutorial revenue** for mid-tier publishers (Packt, mid-Apress, mid-Manning) — the books are inferior on average but free or near-free.
- **Hallucinated content in AI-assisted books** is a brand-equity risk. Publishers that under-invest in fact-checking pipelines lose credibility quickly.
- **Open-access economics squeeze** academic-book margins.

### 7.5 Opportunities
- AI-augmented editorial pipelines let publishers ship *more, faster, in more languages* — offsetting unit-margin pressure with volume + reach.
- *AI-readable* book metadata (machine-actionable APIs, structured TOCs, queryable code listings) creates a new licensing layer with course platforms and AI tutor companies.
- **Companion AI tutors** trained on a single book + author voice become a premium add-on. Stripe Press and Pragmatic Bookshelf are best positioned to lead.

## 8. Self-publishing disruption forecast

| Year | Self-pub share of *beginner* AI/Web3 titles | Self-pub share of *advanced* AI/Web3 titles | Self-pub share of *quantum* titles |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | ~40% | ~12% | ~5% |
| 2027 | ~50% | ~15% | ~7% |
| 2028 | ~60% | ~18% | ~10% |
| 2030 | ~70% | ~22% | ~12% |
| 2035 | (unclear; AI-generated and self-pub blur) | ~25-30% | ~15-20% |

**Implication:** Trade publishers should *cede* the beginner shelf to self-pub and AI, and concentrate on the senior-practitioner, academic-monograph, executive-briefing, and certification-preparation tiers, where editorial value and trust signals justify price premium.

## 9. Academic publishing transformation

- **Open-access mandates** make traditional monograph models unsustainable for many subjects. Academic publishers compensate through:
  - Companion practitioner-trade editions
  - Course-platform licensing
  - Cert-prep deals
  - Conference-proceedings to monograph pipelines
- **Conference proceedings** (LNCS, NeurIPS workshop volumes, ACM proceedings) compete more directly with monographs. Springer and ACM Books are best positioned to use proceedings as a *funnel* into monograph and reference-book commissioning.
- **Generational textbook commissions** become *the* strategic asset for academic presses. The 5-7 anchor textbooks per decade in each major sub-field will dominate course adoption and resulting revenue.

---

## 10. Forecast confidence and audit notes

- 2026-2027 forecasts are largely deterministic from already-committed regulation and standardization. Confidence is high.
- 2028 forecasts depend on the rate of foundation-model progress and the pace of agentic-system maturation. Confidence is medium-high.
- 2030 forecasts depend on whether AI hits another methodological breakthrough vs. a plateau. Confidence is medium.
- 2035 forecasts are *directional*. Treat the table above as a thinking framework, not a prediction.
