# Phase 4 — Market Gap Analysis

> *Where is supply mismatched to demand — and which mismatches are the most economically actionable?*

A gap is not just "no book exists." A gap is a *triangulation*: high latent demand × low current supply × an addressable, willing-to-pay buyer cohort × a defensible shelf life. Plenty of empty topics exist that are empty for good reason. This phase isolates the *actionable* gaps.

---

## 1. Scoring rubric

Six axes, each 1–10. Composite Opportunity Score (COS):

```
COS = 0.25·MO + 0.20·RP + 0.15·AI + 0.10·RR + 0.20·LV + 0.10·SC
```

| Axis | Definition | What pulls the score up | What pulls the score down |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MO** Market Opportunity | Size × accessibility of the buyer cohort | Mandated buyer (compliance), large dev base, enterprise budget | Hobbyist-only, niche jurisdiction, no procurement vehicle |
| **RP** Revenue Potential | Volume × price × shelf life × spinoffs | Hardcover monograph at $80+, course adoption, cert tie-in | Free-tier doc dominance, vendor freebie competition |
| **AI** Academic Impact | Course adoption probability + citation potential | Curriculum-stable, syllabus-fit, cite-able | Vendor-specific, paper-form alternative exists |
| **RR** Research Relevance | Frontier proximity, novelty, methodological contribution | New subfield, citable monograph format | Settled topic, derivative |
| **LV** Longevity | Years until 50% obsolescence | Standards-anchored, theory-anchored | Framework-of-the-month |
| **SC** Commercial Scalability | Translation, course, certification, video, enterprise license spinoffs | Multilingual, certifiable, course-eligible | Single-jurisdiction, single-language |

**Interpretation bands:**
- **COS ≥ 8.5** — *Anchor commission.* Drop other things; ship a flagship in 2026-2027.
- **7.5 – 8.4** — *Strong commission.* Pipeline candidate.
- **6.5 – 7.4** — *Tactical commission.* Niche, regional, or velocity-play candidate.
- **< 6.5** — *Skip or watch.* Unless strategic adjacency demands it.

## 4. Hidden / contrarian opportunities

These are slots not visible to most acquisitions editors because they cross conventional category lines.

1. **AI-for-quantum-control** (not QML). RL for pulse shaping, ML for QEC decoders, ML for calibration. The *real* AI×Quantum intersection. No book exists. (See Phase 3, §4.3.)
2. **Cryptographic agility** as an engineering discipline distinct from PQC. Re-keying, algorithm rotation, hybrid TLS, certificate-lifecycle in a post-PQC world.
3. **"Compiling for the fault-tolerant quantum computer."** A book in the *Engineering a Compiler* tradition for FT quantum compilers. 5-7 year shelf life; near-zero competition.
4. **Multi-agent reliability engineering.** SRE for agent fleets; observability, eval-as-code, rollback. Category-creating.
5. **AI eDiscovery and AI litigation.** Legal-tech adjacency; discovery of model outputs and training data. Wiley-OUP combined-imprint slot.
6. **Defense AI engineering** (legal-export-aware). The audience exists; the books do not. Caution: dual-use export control complexity.
7. **AI-for-procurement.** Government procurement of AI is a multi-trillion-dollar reform. Reference handbook is missing.
8. **Generational maintenance of AI systems.** Drift, deprecation, model EoL, dataset decay. The *operations book* of the 2030s.
9. **Model-serving on consumer hardware.** Apple-on-device, Snapdragon, Mac Studio inference farms — economics is compelling and growing.
10. **AI-and-the-Substack-newsletter as canonical text.** A meta-publishing observation: certain Substacks (Eugene Yan, Chip Huyen-class, Lilian Weng-class) are the *de facto* textbook. Trade publishers should commission the canonical book *from* these authors before competitors do.

## 8. Government and policy publication gaps

Distinct buyer cohorts. Government publishing tends to under-pay per copy but *over-buy* in volume via institutional licensing. Often the highest *aggregate* revenue per title.

1. **OMB M-24-10 & federal AI use inventories** — engineering reference and policy reader.
2. **State-level AI procurement playbooks** — each large US state will need one.
3. **AI in public-benefit administration** — SNAP, unemployment, child welfare AI cases (high stakes, low documentation).
4. **AI in election administration** — non-partisan reference, urgently under-supplied.
5. **Critical-infrastructure AI assurance** — CISA-aligned reference.
6. **AI in international trade controls** — export, BIS, ITAR, EAR, dual-use AI.
7. **AI in international humanitarian law** — autonomous weapons, ICRC-aligned.
8. **PQC national migration plans** — comparative reference: US (CNSA 2.0 / NSM-10), UK NCSC, German BSI, French ANSSI, Australian ASD, Japanese NICT.
9. **National AI workforce strategies** — comparative.
10. **Sovereign-AI infrastructure** — funding, procurement, foundation-model commissioning.

## 9. The single highest-ROI insight from the gap analysis

**The AI-assurance × PQC-migration × cert-prep triangle is structurally the highest-ROI publishing program of 2026-2030.**

- All three legs are *regulation-driven* — buyers are mandated, not discretionary.
- All three legs are *cert-eligible* — book + workbook + practice-test + course bundles work.
- All three legs *compound* — an organization buying any one usually needs the other two.
- Authoring talent overlaps (security architects who do both PQC and AI assurance).
- Multi-language editions are unusually high-ROI because every regulated jurisdiction (EU, UK, JP, KR, SG, AU, UAE, KSA, IN) demands its own reference.

A publisher who commissions a *unified* "Trust & Assurance for AI and Cryptography" series in 2026 — say, 12 titles across 3 years, 5 languages each — has a defensible 5-10 year revenue moat unique in the entire technical publishing landscape.
